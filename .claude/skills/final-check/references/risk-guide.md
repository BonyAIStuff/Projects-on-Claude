# Risk guide

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice. When something is genuinely borderline (a serious allegation, a takedown threat), tell the creator to get a lawyer's view rather than guessing.

## Copyright

**Music and SFX**
- Every audio file needs a known source and licence. Accept: Pixabay (Pixabay Content License: free commercial use, no attribution required); CC0; CC BY with the credit placed in the YouTube description; tracks the creator licensed (e.g. a paid library subscription) with proof in `assets/CREDITS.md`; audio the creator recorded or that was generated for the project (note "self-made" in CREDITS).
- Reject: commercial songs, "no copyright" uploads with no licence, CC NonCommercial (the channel is monetised or may be), anything with an unknown source.
- `onscreen_text.py` lists audio files without a CREDITS file beside them. Each one needs an entry, or it's a finding.

**News articles and screenshots**
- Showing a headline or a few lines to comment on, critique or explain them is commentary. Keep it that way: show only the lines you discuss, add your own analysis, credit the publication and date on screen, never show a whole article or read it out in full.
- Paywalled content: quote briefly; don't reproduce the article.
- Logos and product images of companies discussed: fine for identification in commentary. Don't make them look like the company endorses or sponsors the video.

**Footage and images**
- Stock or B-roll: same rule as audio (known licence, commercial use). Clips from other creators' videos or TV: brief, commented on, credited; otherwise flag.

## Claims about companies

- **Facts** must come from a source and be attributed when they're not common knowledge: "According to <publication>, <date> …", "<Company>'s annual report shows …".
- **Opinions** must read as opinions: "I think", "my read is", "this looks like". Flag sentences that state an interpretation as fact ("they're scamming merchants").
- **Allegations of wrongdoing** (fraud, illegal, cheating, cartel, scam) about a named company or person: critical unless backed by a court ruling, regulator action or a reputable report that says the same thing, and attributed to it. Suggest safer wording ("faced criticism for", "regulators are examining").
- **Financial advice**: no "buy/sell/invest in <stock>" or price predictions as instructions. If the video discusses stocks, IPOs or investing, add an on-screen or description line: "For education only, not financial advice."
- **Forward-looking claims** ("will", "is going to") about a company: soften to "could"/"may", or attribute.
- Numbers about private companies (valuation, revenue) must cite who reported them.

## Platform policy

- **Honest packaging**: the title and thumbnail promise must be delivered in the video. No fake quotes or doctored headlines in thumbnails.
- **Paid promotion**: if any brand paid, gifted or sponsored anything shown, YouTube's "includes paid promotion" box and an on-screen/description disclosure are required; Instagram needs the Paid partnership label. Ask the creator if there's any brand mention.
- **AI or altered content**: if realistic AI-generated or altered visuals or voice appear (a synthetic person, a realistic fake event, a cloned voice), YouTube's altered/synthetic content disclosure applies and Instagram's AI label may apply. Motion graphics, captions and recreated article layouts don't need it.
- **Recreated articles**: an article recreated for style (not a real screenshot) must not pass as a real publication's page; use the real screenshot or label it clearly.
- **Misleading edits**: no cutting a quote so it says the opposite of what was said.
