# Copy style — titles, captions, CTAs

Set by the creator on 2026-10-31. The words around the video should sound like the video: a smart friend explaining business, confident and a little provocative, never sloppy.

## Basics
- **Language:** English. Simple words; explain any jargon in a few words ("MDR, the fee shops pay per payment").
- **Emojis:** at most 1–2 per post, only as markers: 👇 before the poll or CTA, 📌 before sources, 🔗 before a link. Never in YouTube titles, never decorative.
- **Numbers:** ₹ before the amount, % with no space, digits for all numbers ("3 reasons", not "three").
- **Never** invent facts, stats, quotes or links. Every number in a description or caption must be in the script's RESEARCH.md with its source.

## YouTube titles: bold and dramatic, but honest
The creator wants drama. Drama earns the click; honesty keeps the viewer, and a title the video doesn't deliver fails `/final-check`.

Formulas:
| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| **The END of X?** | "The END of Free UPI?" |
| **X is LYING to you / Nobody tells you** | "Nobody Tells You Who Pays This Fee" |
| **Why X secretly…** | "Why Zomato Secretly WANTS You to Pay More" |
| **The ₹X trick behind Y** | "The ₹10 Trick Behind Every Food Order" |
| **X just changed everything for Y** | "This UPI Rule Just Changed Everything for Shops" |
| **How X makes money from…** | "How PhonePe Plans to Make Money From Free Payments" |

Rules:
- Under ~60 characters so it isn't cut off; keyword in the first 3–4 words.
- One or two CAPS words for punch, never the whole title. At most one "?" or "!", never both.
- A question mark when the claim is a possibility, not a fact ("The END of Free UPI?" is fine; "Free UPI Is DEAD" is not, unless it is).
- Never put words in a real person's or company's mouth in quotes, and never imply wrongdoing the video doesn't source ("LYING" only about an idea or a myth, never about a named company).

## Thumbnail text
≤ 4 words that add to the title rather than repeat it ("WHO PAYS?" next to "The END of Free UPI?"). Archivo 800, newsprint background or the creator's face, one highlighter-yellow word, red only as a hand-drawn circle or arrow.

## YouTube description formula
```
<Hook line: the title's promise in one sentence.>

👇 <The debatable question.> Vote in the comments: A) <option> or B) <option>

In this video:
<2–3 short lines on what they'll understand>

Chapters
0:00 <Cold open>
0:25 <Chapter 1>
...

📌 Sources
<publication — headline — date — link, one per line>

Follow for one business strategy explained every week. Share this with a friend who runs a business.
```

## YouTube pinned comment
```
👇 <The debatable question>
A) <option>
B) <option>
Reply with A or B, and tell me why.
```

## Instagram Reels caption formula
```
<Line 1: the hook, the most surprising sentence from the Reel (shows before "more")>

<1–2 lines: the core insight in plain words>

👇 <The debatable question>: A) <option> or B) <option>? Vote in the comments.

Full breakdown on YouTube (link in bio). Save this for later and send it to someone who needs it. Follow for more business strategies, explained simply.

#<specific> #<specific> #<topic> #<broad>
```
- The first line decides whether anyone taps "more": make it the Reel's strongest sentence, not a greeting.
- Hashtags: 3–5, specific beats generic (#upi #digitalpayments #businessstrategy over #viral #trending). Same hashtags on YouTube Shorts as 2–3 `#tags` in the description.

## The recurring CTAs
Use all four on every post, in this order of importance, each phrased once:
1. **Vote A/B in the comments**: the video's poll question, repeated in the caption and the pinned comment.
2. **Follow / subscribe**: "Follow for one business strategy explained every week." (YouTube: "Subscribe for…")
3. **Save / share**: "Save this for later" / "Send this to a friend who runs a business."
4. **Full video on YouTube**: on Reels and Shorts only. "Full breakdown on YouTube, link in bio."
