## Part A — Research brief (researcher + MBA view)

### A1. What the endowment effect is, in one line
We value something more **once it feels like ours**, even if we've owned it for two minutes. Selling
it feels like a loss, and losses hurt more than equal gains feel good (loss aversion).
**Key extension (the bit your video is about):** you don't need to *own* it. Touching, holding,
using or even *imagining* owning it creates "psychological ownership", and that alone raises what
you're willing to pay (R2, R3).

**Analogy:** a rented flat vs your own flat. Same walls. The day you hang your photo on it, a
cracked tile becomes "my tile". Stores try to get you to hang the photo before you've paid.

### A2. The evidence (what the statistics say)
| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Owners demand ~2× what buyers will pay for the same mug | ~$7 vs ~$3 (R1) | Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler, *J. Political Economy* 1990 |
| Merely touching an item raises perceived ownership **and** valuation | qualitative (R2) | Peck & Shu, *J. Consumer Research* 2009 |
| Holding a mug 30 s vs 10 s → significantly higher bids | 144 people (R3) | Wolf, Arkes & Muhanna, *Judgment & Decision Making* 2008 |
| Shopping on a touchscreen vs a mouse → ~50% higher valuation | ~$68 vs ~$47 (R4) | Brasel & Gips, *J. Consumer Psychology* (via Boston College) |
| Assembling it yourself → pay ~63% more (IKEA effect) | 63% (R5) | Norton, Mochon & Ariely (2011/2012) |
| Shoppers who prefer stores *because* they can touch and feel | 85% (R6) | TimeTrade survey, 1,000 US consumers, 2015 |
| Touch & feel = top reason to pick a store over online (clothing) | 56% (R6b) | Mood Media, 10,000 shoppers, 2019 |

**Researcher's caveat (say it on camera, it builds trust):** later work (Plott & Zeiler) argues part
of the mug gap comes from how the experiment was run. The effect is real but smaller and messier in
some settings. It's strongest for things you can touch and use (R4: stronger for a sweatshirt than a
city tour).

### A4. Other ways brands raise sales (beyond endowment) — MBA toolkit
| Lever | How it works | Indian example | Link to endowment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free trial → loss aversion** | Get used to it, then losing it hurts | Spotify/YouTube Premium, Netflix trials; CIRP: 72% of Spotify trialists converted (R15) | Same family: you "own" the service |
| **IKEA effect** | Effort = love | IKEA Hyderabad/Navi Mumbai, customise-your-shoe, build-your-pizza | Labour creates ownership (R5) |
| **Decoy / anchoring** | Add a bad-value option to make the target look good | iPhone storage tiers; cinema popcorn sizes | Separate bias |

### A5. How e-retail fights back (and yes, they *can* do it)
The key line from the research: Peck & Shu found **ownership imagery works even without touch**, and
said so as advice to online retailers (R2). So online brands simulate the three triggers: **touch,
use, imagine**.

| Tactic | What it simulates | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Virtual try-on / AR** (Lenskart 3D try-on, Nykaa & Myntra ModiFace, Flipkart beauty VTO) | "On my face" | Myntra Beauty: **2× conversion, 1.5× consideration** in makeup (R11, company claim); Snap: **72%** more likely to buy after AR try-on (R13); Fielmann: **2×** more store bookings after virtual try-on (R16) |
| **3D / "view in your room"** (IKEA, Pepperfry, Shopify stores) | "In my home" | Shopify: 3D/AR **up to 250%** conversion lift; Gunner Kennels **+40%** conversion, **–5%** returns (R12) |
| **Touchscreens themselves** | Swiping = near-touch | Touchscreen shoppers valued items **~50% higher** than mouse users (R4) |
| **Free returns / try & buy / trials** | "Use it first" | Wakefit 100-night trial (R17); Amazon ran Prime Try Before You Buy 2018–Jan 2025 (F4) |
| **Home try-on** | Real touch, at home | Warby Parker called its home try-on conversion "exceptionally high" in its IPO filing (F5) |
| **Ownership language** | Imagine owning | "Your bag", "Your wishlist", "Imagine this in your living room" (Peck & Shu imagery finding, R2) |
| **Personalisation / customisation** | IKEA effect online | Monogram, build-your-own (R5 logic) |
| **Phygital — just open stores** | The real thing | Online-first brands (Lenskart, Nykaa, Warby Parker) opening stores; Warby is even ending home try-on in favour of stores + virtual try-on (F5) |

**The cost of NOT having touch:** Indian fashion e-commerce returns run ~**30–35%**, and >**40%** of
apparel/footwear returns are due to size (R14). That's the bill e-retail pays for "no touch".

**MBA twist for the video:** touch is expensive. Decathlon India made revenue of **₹4,133 cr** in FY25
but swung to a **₹65 cr loss** (R9) — huge stores with sports courts cost money. So the strategy isn't
free; it's a bet that ownership feelings pay for the floor space.

### A6. "Spot the trap" — how a viewer protects themselves
1. The "cooling-off" rule: put it down, walk out, decide in 24 hours. Ownership fades when you
   aren't touching it (R3 shows it builds in seconds, so it also fades).
2. Ask "would I buy this if I'd never held it?" (flip seller → buyer mindset, R1).
3. Treat free trials as a calendar reminder, not a gift.
4. On apps: notice "your bag", countdown timers and try-ons are doing the store's job.

## Analogies
1. **Rented flat vs own flat** — maps to psychological ownership: same object, different value once it's "mine".
2. **Holding a friend's newborn** — 30 seconds in your arms and you don't want to hand them back; maps to touch duration (R3).
3. **Cricket bat at Decathlon nets** — you've hit three shots with it; it's now *your* bat; maps to use → ownership.
4. **Chai stall regular** — the cup isn't yours, but "my stall" — maps to ownership without legal ownership.
5. **Gym trial week** — once you have "your" locker and routine, cancelling feels like losing something; maps to free trial + loss aversion (R15).

## Viewer objections
1. "Isn't Apple just showing products because people want to see them?" — Yes, and that's the point: the display is designed so you *handle* them, not just see them (screen tilt F1, everything unlocked).
2. "If touch sells so well, why is Decathlon making a loss?" — Touch costs floor space; FY25 loss of ₹65 cr shows it's a bet, not free money (R9).
3. "So online stores just lose?" — No: touchscreens, try-ons and trials recreate part of it (R4, R11, R13).
4. "Isn't this just 'I like nice things'?" — The mug study used identical cheap mugs given at random; only ownership changed (R1).
5. "Is this manipulation?" — In 2003 the Illinois attorney general literally warned holiday shoppers about being asked to hold items (F2). Let viewers decide (poll).

## Spot it yourself
1. **Car showroom test drive** — "take it for the weekend".
2. **Lenskart / Nykaa try-on in the app** — the camera puts the product on *your* face.
3. **IKEA "view in your room"** and Pepperfry 3D — it's in *your* living room.
4. **OTT free trial** — Netflix/YouTube Premium/Spotify: you get used to no ads (R15).
5. **"Your bag" / "Saved for you"** on Myntra, Amazon, Flipkart — language that assigns ownership before payment.
6. **Wakefit 100-night trial** — 100 nights on it and it's your bed (R17).
